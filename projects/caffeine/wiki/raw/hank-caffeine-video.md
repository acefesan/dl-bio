## Why This Matters for This Project

The existing project wiki correctly starts with adenosine receptors, because that is the main physiological mechanism in humans. Hank's video usefully widens the frame: caffeine is also a plant secondary metabolite, an evolved ecological weapon, a convergently evolved purine alkaloid, a pollinator manipulation molecule, a cultural attractor, and an industrial chemical.

That broader frame matters because the caffeine epigenome project should not accidentally become "[ADORA](../concepts/adenosine-receptors.md) expression only." Receptors are the first human-response layer, not the whole caffeine story.

## Open Caveats

- I did not find an official source bibliography from Hank/vlogbrothers for this specific video. The source dossier above is reconstructive: sources that support or nuance the claims, not necessarily the exact sources Hank used.
- The video uses a compressed popular-science line that caffeine "does the same things" in insects and humans. The invertebrate literature is more nuanced: [ryanodine receptor and phosphodiesterase effects](../concepts/secondary-caffeine-targets.md) are well established, while direct adenosine-receptor interaction in invertebrates is less settled.
- The "plants use caffeine by mimicking adenosine" line needs care. Plant allelopathy and toxicity are supported, but the molecular mechanism in plants should be treated as a follow-up question rather than a settled one-liner.
