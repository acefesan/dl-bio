# GWAS, EWAS, and Pharmacogenomics

## Summary

Population genetics shows that caffeine biology is not only liver metabolism. GWAS loci include metabolism genes, brain and circadian genes, ion channels, appetite/energy-homeostasis genes, and chromatin-related genes.

Keep exposure definitions sharp. Coffee intake, tea intake, plasma caffeine, and synthetic caffeine exposure are related but not interchangeable traits.

For a focused map of caffeine-sensitivity loci and how to interpret them, see [caffeine sensitivity genetics](caffeine-sensitivity-genetics.md).

## Pharmacokinetic Loci

Important caffeine metabolism and exposure loci include:

- AHR
- CYP1A1/CYP1A2
- ABCG2
- POR
- GCKR

These help explain differences in caffeine metabolism and plasma caffeine levels.

## Pharmacodynamic and Tissue-Relevant Loci

The proposal highlights loci such as:

- BDNF
- SLC6A4
- RORA
- HCN2
- CACNA2D2
- MC4R
- SORCS2
- BRWD1

These point to nervous-system, circadian, calcium-channel, metabolic, and chromatin-remodeling biology.

## EWAS Signal

Coffee consumption EWAS results include CpGs near genes such as AHRR, F2RL3, GFI1, HDAC4, and PHGDH. A key caution is confounding, especially overlap with smoking-associated CpGs.
