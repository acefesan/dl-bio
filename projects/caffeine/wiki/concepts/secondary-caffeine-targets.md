# Secondary Caffeine Targets

## Summary

At ordinary human coffee doses, caffeine is mainly an [adenosine receptor](adenosine-receptors.md) antagonist. At higher concentrations, caffeine can affect several other target systems. These are **secondary targets** for this project: important, but dose-sensitive.

The big four in the proposal are:

- phosphodiesterases,
- ryanodine receptors,
- ATM/ATR checkpoint kinases,
- mTOR-linked growth pathways.

## Phosphodiesterases

**Phosphodiesterases**, often abbreviated **PDEs**, are enzymes that break down cyclic nucleotide messengers such as cAMP and cGMP.

If caffeine inhibits PDEs, cAMP/cGMP signals last longer. That can amplify PKA/PKG signaling and make receptor-driven signals look stronger or more persistent.

Project caution: PDE inhibition usually requires higher caffeine concentrations than normal coffee exposure.

## Ryanodine Receptors

**Ryanodine receptors**, often abbreviated **RyR**, are calcium-release channels on internal calcium stores such as the endoplasmic or sarcoplasmic reticulum.

If caffeine activates RyR channels, calcium leaves the internal store and enters the cytosol. That can activate calcium-dependent pathways such as CaMKII, HDAC export, MEF2, and NFAT.

Project caution: RyR activation is central in muscle and calcium biology, but many caffeine experiments use supraphysiological concentrations.

## ATM/ATR

**ATM** and **ATR** are checkpoint kinases that help cells respond to DNA damage and replication stress.

If caffeine inhibits ATM/ATR, cells can become less able to pause and repair after DNA damage. This is why caffeine appears in some radiosensitization and cancer-biology literature.

Project caution: ATM/ATR inhibition is not the first explanation for everyday caffeine effects.

## mTOR

**mTOR** is a central growth and nutrient-sensing kinase. It integrates energy status, growth factors, amino acids, and stress signals to regulate protein synthesis, growth, and autophagy.

If caffeine inhibits mTOR-linked signaling, it can push cells toward autophagy or altered growth-state programs.

Project caution: mTOR effects often appear in high-dose cell experiments, so dose and cell type matter.

## Why This Matters for Epigenomics

These targets point to different transcriptional and chromatin readouts:

- PDE effects point toward cAMP/cGMP, PKA, PKG, and CREB.
- RyR effects point toward calcium, CaMKII, HDAC export, MEF2, and NFAT.
- ATM/ATR effects point toward DNA damage response programs.
- mTOR effects point toward autophagy, growth, and stress-state programs.
