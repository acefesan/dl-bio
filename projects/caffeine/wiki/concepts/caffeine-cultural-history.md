# Caffeine Cultural History

## Summary

The weird human fact in Hank's video is not only that plants repeatedly evolved caffeine. It is that humans repeatedly learned to prepare caffeinated drinks from unrelated plants across different regions.

For this project, cultural history matters because exposure labels such as coffee, tea, mate, cacao, kola, guarana, yaupon, and soda are chemically and historically different. They should not be collapsed into a single "caffeine" variable unless the analysis is explicitly about caffeine dose.

## Major Plant-Based Caffeine Traditions

| Tradition | Plant/source | Broad region |
|---|---|---|
| Tea | Camellia sinensis | East Asia |
| Coffee | Coffea spp. | Horn of Africa / Arabia |
| Cacao beverage | Theobroma cacao | Mesoamerica |
| Kola | Cola spp. | West Africa |
| Guarana | Paullinia cupana | Amazon basin |
| Yerba mate | Ilex paraguariensis | Southern South America |
| Yaupon | Ilex vomitoria | Southeastern North America |

This table is a conceptual map, not a complete ethnobotanical history. Each row has deeper Indigenous, colonial, trade, and industrial histories that should be handled carefully if the project expands in that direction.

## Yaupon

Yaupon is especially useful for the video's frame because it is a North American holly used historically as a caffeinated drink. Modern summaries often describe it as North America's only native caffeine-containing plant, though the exact wording varies between "plant," "beverage plant," and "source."

## Industrial Caffeine

Modern beverages can be caffeinated without using a traditional caffeine plant. Commercial caffeine can be extracted from decaffeination streams or produced synthetically. This matters because industrial caffeine decouples human caffeine exposure from the plant matrix that historically delivered it.

Key source:

- [IARC/NCBI Bookshelf caffeine production chapter](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK507027/)

## Implication for Data Analysis

For epidemiology, GWAS, and EWAS:

- "coffee intake" is not equivalent to "caffeine dose";
- "tea intake" includes L-theanine and tea polyphenols;
- "soda/energy drink caffeine" may be synthetic or purified caffeine;
- "plasma caffeine" reflects metabolism, timing, genotype, and dose;
- "caffeine-associated trait" may capture behavior, pharmacokinetics, or pharmacodynamics.
