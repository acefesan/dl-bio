# Xanthines

## Summary

**Xanthine** is a purine-derived molecule in normal metabolism. **Methylxanthines** are xanthine molecules with one or more methyl groups attached. Caffeine is a methylxanthine: specifically **1,3,7-trimethylxanthine**.

This is the chemistry bridge between plant caffeine and human adenosine signaling. Xanthines are close enough to purine biology that methylxanthines can interact with enzymes and receptors built for purine-related molecules.

## Core Definitions

| Term | Meaning | Caffeine relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Xanthine | A purine-derived base produced in purine metabolism | The chemical scaffold that caffeine is built from |
| Methyl group | A small chemical group, `CH3` | Adding methyl groups changes xanthine's biological behavior |
| Methylxanthine | A xanthine with methyl groups attached | Caffeine, theobromine, theophylline, and paraxanthine are methylxanthines |
| Trimethylxanthine | A xanthine with three methyl groups | Caffeine is 1,3,7-trimethylxanthine |

## Related Methylxanthines

| Molecule | Chemical shorthand | Common context |
|---|---|---|
| Caffeine | 1,3,7-trimethylxanthine | coffee, tea, guarana, mate, kola, yaupon |
| Theobromine | 3,7-dimethylxanthine | cacao/chocolate |
| Theophylline | 1,3-dimethylxanthine | tea; historical airway pharmacology |
| Paraxanthine | 1,7-dimethylxanthine | main human caffeine metabolite |

## Why Methylation Matters

Adding methyl groups changes solubility, receptor binding, enzyme interactions, and metabolism. This is why caffeine, theobromine, and theophylline are related but do not feel or behave identically.

## Link to ADORA Biology

Caffeine's methylxanthine structure lets it bind adenosine receptor proteins without activating them. In human gene-symbol language, those receptors are encoded by the [ADORA gene family](adenosine-receptors.md).
