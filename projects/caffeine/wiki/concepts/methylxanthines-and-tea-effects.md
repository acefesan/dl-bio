# Methylxanthines and Tea Effects

## Summary

Caffeine is one member of a family of [methylxanthines](xanthines.md). Tea, cacao, and related beverages often contain mixtures of caffeine, theobromine, theophylline, and non-xanthine compounds such as L-theanine. This helps explain why "caffeine-containing drink" is not one uniform exposure.

## Related Methylxanthines

| Molecule | Common association | Project relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Caffeine | coffee, tea, guarana, kola, yaupon, yerba mate | main [ADORA](adenosine-receptors.md) antagonist and project focus |
| Theobromine | cacao/chocolate | related methylxanthine, weaker stimulant profile |
| Theophylline | tea, historical asthma pharmacology | related methylxanthine with stronger airway/cardiac pharmacology |
| Paraxanthine | main human caffeine metabolite | may mediate some downstream effects of caffeine metabolism |

## Tea Is a Mixture

Green tea and black tea are both produced from Camellia sinensis, but processing changes chemistry and sensory profile. Tea also contains L-theanine, an amino acid that has been studied in combination with caffeine.

Human randomized studies and meta-analyses suggest caffeine plus L-theanine can improve some attention/task-switching measures and may feel different from caffeine alone. That supports Hank's "more chill while aware" framing, but the effect is task-, dose-, and timing-dependent.

## Implication for the Epigenome Project

If the project ever compares "coffee intake," "tea intake," and "plasma caffeine," keep exposure definitions separate:

- pure caffeine,
- coffee as a chemically complex food,
- tea as caffeine plus L-theanine/polyphenols,
- cacao as mostly theobromine plus some caffeine,
- synthetic caffeine added to beverages.

This matters for GWAS, EWAS, and Mendelian randomization because coffee intake, tea intake, and circulating caffeine can point in different biological directions.
