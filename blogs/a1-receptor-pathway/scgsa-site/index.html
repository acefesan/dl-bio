A reading companion

Scoring gene sets in single cells, from first principles

Wang & Thakar (2024) compare seven ways to turn a list of genes into one number per cell, and show that most of them can be fooled. This site takes each method apart: what it computes, why, and where it breaks in the paper's four simulated scenarios.

The paper

Comparative analysis of single-cell pathway scoring methods and a novel approach. Ruoqiao H. Wang and Juilee Thakar. NAR Genomics and Bioinformatics 6(3), lqae124, 2024. Read it here (open access, CC BY-NC 4.0).

The one idea to hold onto

Every method answers the same question — "is this gene set more active in this cell?" — but none of them can look only at the gene set. To say "more active" you need a reference: other genes in the same cell, other cells, or a background of control genes. Whatever a method compares against is also what can fool it.

The paper's four scenarios are four different ways of disturbing that reference. The most important one, scenario 3, changes nothing about the genes you are scoring. It only makes one group of cells express about 2% more genes overall, and six of the seven methods then report a difference that isn't there.

The seven methods

Grouped by what they compare the gene set against — the most useful way to predict how each one fails.

Magnitude · vs control genes
AddModuleScore
Mean of the set minus the mean of expression-matched random genes.
Magnitude · vs cell total
SCSE
The set's share of everything the cell expresses.
Rank · top of the list
AUCell
How many set genes appear among the cell's top 5% of genes.
Rank · Mann–Whitney
UCell
A Mann–Whitney U statistic on the set's ranks, capped at rank 1500.
Rank · random walk
ssGSEA
A running-sum walk down the whole ranked gene list, one cell at a time.
Rank + detection
JASMINE
Rank among expressed genes, blended with an odds ratio of detection.
Covariance · within the set
scPS
The paper's own method: PCA on the set's genes, weighted by variance, times mean expression.

What I checked, so you can trust this site

  • Every formula here was read from the installed package source (Seurat 5.5.1, AUCell 1.28.0, UCell 2.10.1, GSVA 2.0.0, irGSEA, and the authors' own scPS_2024.R, scPS_2025.R and comparison scripts), not from the paper's prose. Where the two disagree, the page says so.
  • The playground's JavaScript versions were checked against the real R packages on identical matrices: UCell, ssGSEA, JASMINE and SCSE agree exactly (r = 1.000000); AddModuleScore and AUCell agree at r ≈ 0.91–0.996, the gap coming only from their random-number draws (control genes and tie-breaks); scPS agrees at r ≥ 0.993 once eigenvector signs are aligned (details on the scPS page).
  • The toy simulator reproduces the paper's headline results — the scenario 3 false positives, scPS's low false-positive rate — but not all its magnitudes, and it does not reproduce ssGSEA's near-total failure. The playground lists what matches and what doesn't.
