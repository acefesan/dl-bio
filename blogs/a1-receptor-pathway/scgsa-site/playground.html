Mechanisms, not magnitudes

The simulator is much smaller than the paper's data (3,000–6,000 genes, not ~20,000) and its exact regime is a guess. It reproduces the scenario 3 false positives, their growth with gene set size, and scPS's immunity. It does not reproduce ssGSEA's near-total failure, or UCell's low scenario-1 false-positive rate. Things to try

  • Scenario 3, null sets: condition-specific share 0%, recovery over 30 sets. Everything except scPS fires.
  • Then Sweep gene set size on the same setting: the "bigger is shadier" curve.
  • Scenario 1, noise 100%: AUCell, JASMINE and SCSE flag gene sets containing no signal gene.
  • Scenario 1 again, with before normalisation: raising the densest genes shrinks every other gene through the library size, and more methods start seeing ghosts.
  • Scenario 2, sparse regime, 20 cells per group: scPS loses power before the rank-based methods do (about 40% against 100% for AUCell, UCell and JASMINE), as the paper found below 50 cells.
  • Scenario 2, gene set size 25, 20 cells: AUCell and JASMINE — the pair the paper recommends for small sparse sets — keep their power while AddModuleScore, SCSE and scPS drop to about a third.
