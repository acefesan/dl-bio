Foundations

The four scenarios

Two scenarios ask "can you find a real signal?" Two ask "can you avoid finding a fake one?" The second pair is where the paper earns its keep.

The shared setup

Take a real single-cell dataset and split its cells at random into two equal groups, control and treatment. Before anything is added, the two groups are biologically identical. Then disturb the treatment group in a controlled way, generate thousands of gene sets whose composition you know, score them with each method, and test every set for a control-vs-treatment difference.

Because the disturbance is known, every "identified" gene set is either a correct call or a false positive. Nothing is left to judgment.

expressed, unchanged expressed, +20% in treatment (signal) expressed only in treatment (condition-specific)

S1 Signal on densely expressed genes

Design. Genes detected in at least 90% of cells (SSD) or 75% (RWSD) — at least 500 of them — get a 20% increase in expression in treatment cells. Everything else is left alone.

What a good method should do. Detect gene sets built from signal genes, and ignore gene sets built from the other genes.

The trap. Signal genes are also the most highly expressed genes in every cell. Anything that scores a gene relative to other genes in the same cell — rank-based methods, and SCSE's share-of-total — is affected when those top genes rise. Genes that did not change get pushed down the ranking or become a smaller share of the total, so gene sets made entirely of unchanged genes can still shift.

What happened (gene set size 100, 200 cells; Table 1). Sensitivity was high for everything except ssGSEA (6–17%) and UCell on SSD (30%). But at 100% noise — gene sets containing no signal gene — JASMINE flagged 74–100% of them, AUCell 45–100%, SCSE 26–40%. AddModuleScore and scPS stayed at 3.5–5%, UCell at 1–12%, ssGSEA under 1%.

S2 Signal on randomly chosen genes

Design. 550 randomly chosen genes get the +20%. Because most genes are sparse, the median signal gene is detected in just 1.75% of cells, against 41.25% in scenario 1.

What it tests. Whether a method can find a real change that is mostly hidden behind zeros. A 20% increase in a gene you almost never observe is nearly invisible cell by cell.

What happened. Everything got harder. Recovery fell faster as noise rose, but most methods still found above 80% of sets at 20% noise. scPS and SCSE suffered most with few cells: PCA needs enough cells to estimate covariance from a mostly-zero matrix, and scPS only exceeded 90% recovery with more than 200 cells per group. False positives were lower than in S1, with JASMINE (6–25%) and AUCell (3–17%) again the highest.

S3 No signal — extra dense genes in treatment

Design. Nothing gets a signal. Instead, about 250 extra genes (~2%) are added to the treatment group only — they are zero in every control cell. In S3 these condition-specific genes are densely expressed. Gene sets are built either without any condition-specific genes, or with a small share of them (~2% of the set).

What a good method should do. A gene set with no condition-specific genes contains only genes that are identical in both groups. The correct answer is "no difference", every time.

The trap. Treatment cells now express more genes. For a rank-based method, those extra genes slot in above the scored genes and push their ranks down. For SCSE, they raise the cell's total and shrink the scored genes' share. For AddModuleScore, they change the per-gene averages the control genes are matched on. The scored genes did not move — their context did.

What happened. This is the paper's central result. Every method except scPS detected gene sets that contained no condition-specific genes: they reported a difference whenever the two groups differed in how many genes they detect. Larger gene sets made it worse. scPS was the only method that stayed quiet, and it did detect sets as their share of condition-specific genes rose — the correct behaviour.

Why this scenario exists

It models something common in real data: groups that genuinely differ in transcriptional breadth. The paper points to cancer cells. The same applies to comparing cell types — neurons detect far more genes per cell than glia. See Our A1 data.

S4 No signal — extra sparse genes in treatment

Design. Identical to S3, except the condition-specific genes are randomly chosen and therefore mostly sparse. Each treatment cell only picks up a few of them.

What happened. The false positives shrank, because the difference in genes detected per cell is small, but did not vanish: in RWSD all methods except scPS still flagged a few gene sets with no condition-specific genes. Correctly detecting sets that do contain condition-specific genes needed a larger share of them than in S3.

What S3 and S4 are really measuring

Put the four side by side and a single variable separates them: how much of each score comes from outside the gene set.

scPS is the only method whose inputs are the gene set's own values and nothing else — the PCA uses only the set's genes, and so does the mean. That, not cleverness, is why it survived scenario 3. It also explains its weakness in S2: with no outside reference, it has nothing to lean on when the set itself is almost all zeros.

"Leans on" above is our reading of each method's formula (see the method pages). The paper reports the outcomes; it does not spell out these mechanisms.
