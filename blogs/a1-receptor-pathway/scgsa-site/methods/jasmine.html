Rank + detection

JASMINE

Split the evidence in two. Among the genes the cell does express, how high do the set's genes rank? And is the set over-represented among expressed genes at all? Average the two, after rescaling each to 0–1 across cells.

The question it asks

JASMINE — Jointly Assessing Signature Mean and INferring Enrichment (Noureen et al. 2022) — was built after its authors showed that bulk methods like ssGSEA break on sparse single-cell data. Its answer to the zero problem is to stop ranking zeros. Zeros are taken out of the ranking and used as a separate kind of evidence: detection.

  • Signature mean: rank only the expressed genes. How high do the expressed set genes sit?
  • Enrichment: is a set gene more likely to be detected than a non-set gene in this cell? An odds ratio from a 2×2 table.

The computation

For cell \(c\), let \(E_c\) be the set of its expressed (nonzero) genes, \(|E_c|\) how many there are, and \(S\) the gene set. Rank the genes in \(E_c\) ascending among themselves, so the highest gets rank \(|E_c|\); \(\operatorname{rank}(g)\) is gene \(g\)'s rank in that list. \(S \cap E_c\) is the set genes that are expressed.

$$\text{RM}_c = \frac{\operatorname{mean}_{g \in S \cap E_c}\operatorname{rank}(g)}{|E_c|} \qquad (0 \text{ if no set gene is expressed})$$

Then the 2×2 detection table for that cell, whose entries are counts of genes (\(n_{11}\) = set genes that are expressed, and so on):

expressednot expressed
in set\(n_{11}\)\(n_{12}\)
not in set\(n_{21}\)\(n_{22}\)
$$\text{OR}_c = \frac{n_{11} \cdot n_{22}}{n_{12} \cdot n_{21}} \qquad (n_{12} \text{ or } n_{21} \text{ set to 1 if zero})$$

Rescale each across all cells being scored, then average. Here \(\text{RM}\) and \(\text{OR}\) stand for the vectors of those values over all cells, and \(\min v\), \(\max v\) are taken over all cells:

$$\text{JASMINE}_c = \tfrac{1}{2}\Big[\operatorname{minmax}(\text{RM})_c + \operatorname{minmax}(\text{OR})_c\Big], \qquad \operatorname{minmax}(v)_c = \frac{v_c - \min v}{\max v - \min v}$$

On our toy cell

The expressed genes are A, B, D, F, H, J. Ranked ascending among themselves: H 1, J 2, D 3, B 4, F 5, A 6. The expressed set genes are B (4) and D (3); G is not expressed and is simply absent.

$$\text{RM} = \frac{(4 + 3)/2}{6} = 0.58$$

Detection table: in the set, 2 expressed (B, D) and 1 not (G); outside it, 4 expressed (A, F, H, J) and 3 not (C, E, I).

$$\text{OR} = \frac{n_{11} n_{22}}{n_{12} n_{21}} = \frac{2 \times 3}{1 \times 4} = 1.5$$

Neither number is the score yet: both are rescaled against every other cell before averaging. A single cell has no JASMINE score on its own.

What the benchmarked code does

The paper uses JASMINE.method = "oddsratio" through irGSEA, whose implementation matches the authors' original JASMINE_V1_11October2021.r (the playground's version agrees with it exactly, r = 1.000000). The heart of it:

subdata = x[x != 0]                           # drop dropouts
DataRanksUpdated = rank(subdata)
FinalRawRank = mean(ranks of set genes) / length(subdata)
...
SigGenesNE[SigGenesNE == 0] = 1
NSigGenesExp[NSigGenesExp == 0] = 1
OR = (SigGenesExp * NSigGenesNE) / (SigGenesNE * NSigGenesExp)
JAS_Scores = (NormalizationJAS(RM) + NormalizationJAS(OR)) / 2
A consequence of min–max scaling

Every cell's score depends on the most extreme cells in the matrix. Add or remove one outlier cell and every other cell's score changes. Comparisons within one scoring run are fine; comparing JASMINE scores across separately scored datasets is not.

Zero policy, and what leaks in

Zeros: removed from the ranking, then counted as evidence in the odds ratio. The most deliberate zero policy of the seven.

From outside the set: twice over. The rank term depends on how many genes the cell expresses and where they sit; the odds ratio depends directly on how many non-set genes are detected (\(n_{21}\)); and min–max scaling depends on every other cell.

On our A1 data

On the 8-gene panel, JASMINE put Amygdala excitatory first with Hippocampal CA4 a whisker behind (0.530 vs. 0.527); by median, CA4 was first. On the 61-gene panel CA4 was 7th. On Tabula Sapiens it was the only method not to rank mural cells first on the 8-gene panel, choosing BEST4⁺ enterocytes. Since its odds ratio counts every non-set gene a cell detects, and HBCA superclusters differ 4.2× in genes detected, its cross-cell-type comparisons carry the scenario 3 confound in two places at once.
