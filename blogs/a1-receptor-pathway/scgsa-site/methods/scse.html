Magnitude · vs cell total

SCSE

What share of everything this cell expresses belongs to the gene set? The simplest method of the seven, and the one whose failure mode is easiest to see.

The question it asks

Single-Cell Signature Explorer (Pont et al. 2019) sidesteps the question of what counts as "high" by making the score a proportion. If a cell devotes a larger slice of its transcriptome to a pathway, the pathway is more active. No bins, no ranks, no control genes.

The computation

$$\text{SCSE}_c = 100 \times \frac{\sum_{g \in S} x_{gc}}{\sum_{g=1}^{G} x_{gc}}$$

Sum the set's expression in the cell, divide by the sum over every gene in the cell, express as a percentage.

On our toy cell

Set sum: \(7.42 + 6.91 + 0 = 14.33\). Cell total over all ten genes: \(8.29 + 7.42 + 6.91 + 7.76 + 5.81 + 6.50 = 42.69\).

$$\text{SCSE} = 100 \times \frac{14.33}{42.69} = 33.6$$

Now imagine the same cell also expressed three extra genes at around 7 each. Its total rises to about 63.7 and the score falls to 22.5, although B, D and G are unchanged. That one line is scenario 3.

What the benchmarked code does

irGSEA's implementation, as the paper calls it:

umi.sum     <- colSums2(my.matrix.subset)
umi.geneset <- colSums2(my.matrix.subset, rows = which(rownames(...) %in% geneset))
scse.result <- umi.geneset / umi.sum * 100

The variable is called umi.sum, but the paper runs irGSEA with slot = "data", so it sums log-normalised values, not UMI counts. A sum of logs is a very different "total" from a sum of molecules — the denominator is dominated by how many genes are detected rather than by how many molecules were captured.

Zero policy, and what leaks in

Zeros: contribute nothing to either sum.

From outside the set: the whole denominator. Every gene in the cell that is not in your set still sets the scale. More genes detected, or any gene rising, and your set's share shrinks.

On our A1 data

On the 8-gene panel, SCSE ranked Hippocampal CA4 2nd — behind Upper rhombic lip, the HBCA supercluster that detects the fewest genes per cell (median 1,987, against 7,604 for CA4). With a sum of log values as the denominator, a cell that detects a quarter as many genes has a far smaller total, and any set genes it does detect become a larger share. This is scenario 3 running in reverse, and it is the most direct sign that cross-cell-type comparisons in your data carry the confound. Details.
